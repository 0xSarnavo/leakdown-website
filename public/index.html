How a run works

01

Read the site

What it sells, to whom, where signup is. Then a crawler walks two clicks from the landing page plus the sitemap, with no AI, so the report can later say which pages nobody found and which links are broken.

02

Build ten prospects

Core customers, adjacent roles, and people who are not the target at all, each fitted to the product. Each gets its own email address, so verification codes and magic links are read and used, not guessed at.

03

Send them in

Each prospect drives a real browser one viewport at a time and thinks aloud at every step until it finishes, walks out with a reason, or runs out of patience. Every step is logged and recorded on video.

04

Climb the ladder

Cheap models vote: half the prospects run on haiku, half on a free model. A mechanical filter keeps only what more than one session cites. A verifier reviews those sessions. A stronger model re-walks the hardest prospect and writes the report you read.

What it has found so far

Three real sites, September 2026. Names withheld until the owners agree.

Four signup buttons with no label

A screen reader announces each as "button" and nothing else. 14 sessions across 5 models cited the same four elements. The page ruler now catches this on the first load.

A workspace form that resets

Submit, the field disables, the page comes back empty. 3 of 26 prospects walked out on that page. The quote in the report above is one of them.

A terms page with empty sections

Section 5, "Data and Privacy", shows sub-headings and no text. Two security-minded prospects left over it, and neither found the compliance detail they came for.

Same prospect, five runs

A prospect who knew the price and the signup path ended the same way, on the same page, 5 of 5 times. A low-tech edge prospect agreed with itself half the time, so a finding one session made earns a second run before the report counts it.

Email walls are walked through

Prospects receive real verification codes and type them in. When no mail arrives, the report says its email verdicts are unverified instead of blaming your site.

Measured, not felt

Beside every prospect's opinion sits a ruler: controls with no accessible name, tap targets under 24px, sideways scroll, a missing viewport tag. Those lines you can verify without trusting anyone.

What it never does

  • Pay, book a meeting, or sign in with Google, GitHub, or SSO. A guard refuses the action itself, on any page. Reaching the wall is the finding. The guard matches labels, so do not point it at a live checkout and assume it cannot buy.
  • Report one voice as truth. A finding only one session made is listed under "seen once, not counted", never presented as verified.
  • Measure your traffic. A simulated prospect stalling is a signal that real visitors could. It is never a percentage of your users or a conversion number, and every report says so.
  • Know your site in advance. Cold prospects arrive knowing nothing, warm ones know the pitch, hot ones know the price and the signup path.
  • Track you here. This site sets no cookies, loads no analytics, no third-party scripts and no external fonts. The request form stores what you type and nothing else. Privacy · Terms.
